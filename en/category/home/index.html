Home & neighbors

  • No unapproved roommates or sublettingLetting someone live with you without notifying the landlord can get you asked to move out. Subletting a room requires the landlord's consent (Civil Code).Local or facility rule
  • Give written notice before moving outWhen you move out, tell the landlord or agent in writing by the deadline in your contract (notice of cancellation). The deadline depends on the contract.Local or facility rule
  • Keep hallways and stairs clearHallways and stairs outside your room are shared areas and evacuation routes in an emergency. A balcony may also be an evacuation route.Local or facility rule
  • Your balcony can be an escape routeBalconies can serve as escape routes in a fire or other emergency. Do not put large or heavy things in front of the partition next to your neighbor. Some buildings ban using fire, such as for barbecues, or hanging futons over the railing. The link is an example from UR rental housing. Check the rules of your building.Manners
  • Know about neighborhood associationsNeighborhood associations (jichikai or chonaikai) are groups that local residents form on their own. Their activities include circulating notices from the city office and others, and members pay the costs. Activities vary by area, so ask your municipality if you are unsure.Manners
  • Keep noise down from night to early morningIn apartment buildings, sound travels easily to the rooms next door, above and below. Take particular care from night until early morning. With musical instruments and audio equipment too, mind the volume and the time of day.Manners
  • Check the rules before you get a petMost apartment buildings ban pets, and even where pets are allowed, the type, size and number are almost always set by the building's rules. Barking, smells and uncollected droppings are common causes of trouble with neighbors.Manners
  • Take off your shoes at the entranceIn Japan it is customary to take off your shoes in the entryway before going inside a home.Manners
