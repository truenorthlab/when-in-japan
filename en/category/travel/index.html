Travel & sightseeing

  • Drive on the left, and always stop at red lights and stop signsIn Japan, vehicles drive on the left side of the road. Stop at red lights, and where there is a stop sign (止まれ), come to a full stop before the stop line and check left and right. When someone is crossing or about to cross at a crosswalk, stop before it and let them go first. Traffic rules and signs in Japan can differ from those in other countries.Law
  • Check the flight rules before flying a droneFlying a drone in Japan may require registering the aircraft. Without permission, you cannot fly around airports, over densely populated districts and in similar areas.Law
  • Check the rules before bringing medicineSome medicines brought from abroad have quantity limits or need procedures in advance. Amphetamines and cannabis cannot be brought in, even for medical treatment.Law
  • Don't take plants or stones from national parksIn special zones of national parks and quasi-national parks, the Natural Parks Act restricts picking designated plants, taking stones or soil, and other activities. You cannot tell by looking which places are restricted, so do not take flowers, branches or stones, and stay on the marked paths.Law
  • Carry your passport while travelingVisitors without a residence card must carry their passport at all times and show it when a police officer or other authorized official asks. There are exceptions, so check the official text for details.Law
  • Don't take shellfish, sea cucumbers and other sea life without permission (poaching)Taking abalone or sea cucumbers without a license or similar authorization is punished, whether or not you are a fisher and regardless of how much you took or where. Taking turban shells, spiny lobsters and other species covered by fishing rights without authorization is also poaching. This is the same even for things you find on the beach or rocky shore. Fishing and clam digging also have rules set by law and by each prefecture, covering the gear you may use, closed areas and seasons, size limits and more. Recreational fishing by people without Japanese nationality is allowed only within a limited range, so check with the prefecture or the fishing boat operator beforehand.Law
  • Don't enter off-limits places, other people's fields or private roadsEntering a place where entry is prohibited, or someone else's fields, without a legitimate reason is prohibited under the Minor Offenses Act. Entering someone's home without permission can also be the crime of trespassing under the Penal Code. Some roads that anyone can walk on are private roads owned by individuals or others rather than the national or local government. Follow any signs closing the road or prohibiting photography. Even for photos or a shortcut, do not go in when there is a sign.Law
  • Take tax-free purchases out of Japan after customs confirmation at departureWhen you shop at a tax-free store, you pay the price including tax. When you leave Japan, you show your passport at a terminal at the airport or seaport to receive customs confirmation, and then the consumption tax is refunded. Get the confirmation before checking in your luggage. If even one item on a receipt is missing, nothing on that receipt is refunded. Consumables eaten or used in Japan are not refunded either. If you do not take items confirmed by customs out of Japan, the consumption tax is collected and you may also face penalties. Check the deadlines and refund methods on the official page or with the store.Law
  • Don't resell concert or sports tickets above face valueUnder the Act on Ensuring Proper Distribution of Tickets for Entertainment Events, reselling qualifying concert, sports and other tickets above face value as a business, without the organizer's consent, is prohibited. Even a single resale can be judged as doing it as a business. Buying tickets in order to resell them is also prohibited. You may be refused entry with a resold ticket. If you can no longer go, use the official resale service approved by the organizer.Law
  • Don't catch wild birds or animalsWild birds and mammals are, as a rule, protected under the Wildlife Protection and Management Act, and catching or harming them without permission is prohibited. Taking bird eggs is also prohibited. This includes familiar birds in towns and parks, such as pigeons, sparrows and crows. Removing a nest with eggs or chicks in it is also prohibited. Do not catch them to eat or keep as pets; just watch them.Law
  • In Biei, Hokkaido, don't walk into the fields on the hillsThe beautiful scenery of Biei's hills is made up entirely of farmers' fields. The fields are the farmers' private property, and entering them without permission is illegal. Snow-covered hills are fields too, with wheat and other crops growing under the snow. Soil carried on your shoes can also harm the crops. Do not drive or walk onto gravel roads or farm roads either. Flying drones and aerial photography in the town are prohibited in principle.Local or facility ruleApplies in: Biei Town, Hokkaido
  • Check the rules on the official site before climbing Mt. FujiDuring the summer climbing season, Yamanashi and Shizuoka prefectures each set rules for the trails on Mt. Fuji. These include advance registration or reservations, an entry fee and limits on passing through at night, and climbers are asked not to do “bullet climbing” (climbing through the night without staying at a mountain hut). The details change from year to year, so check the official site before you climb.Local or facility ruleApplies in: Yamanashi and Shizuoka (Mt. Fuji)
  • In Gion, don't stop geiko or maiko or photograph them without askingIn the Gion-machi Minamigawa district of Gion, Kyoto, residents, teahouses, the geiko association and others ask visitors to follow good manners. Do not stop, touch, follow or photograph geiko and maiko without permission as they walk by. Photographing on private property such as in front of shops, and entering it without permission, have also become problems. This is a place where people live their daily lives, not a theme park. Police officers may come when residents call them.Local or facility ruleApplies in: Higashiyama Ward, Kyoto City, Kyoto (Gion-machi Minamigawa district and surroundings)
  • On designated mountain trails, submit a climbing plan before you goBy ordinance, Nagano Prefecture designates trails on mountains with a high risk of accidents as "designated mountain trails" and requires a climbing plan to be submitted before using them. Gifu Prefecture also has mountains where submitting a climbing notice is mandatory. Submitting online is recommended, and you can also send it by post or drop it in the box at the trailhead. Some trailheads do not have a box. On the day of the climb, carry the plan you made with you.Local or facility ruleApplies in: Designated mountain trails in Nagano, Gifu and other prefectures
  • Reserve ahead for large luggage on the ShinkansenOn the Tokaido, Sanyo, Kyushu and Nishi-Kyushu Shinkansen, luggage over a certain size needs a reserved seat with a baggage space. Check the official guide for the size limits.Local or facility rule
  • Follow ski resort rules and don't casually leave the managed areaOutside a ski resort's managed area is winter mountain terrain, completely different from groomed slopes. There are hard-to-predict dangers such as sudden weather changes, avalanches, getting lost and hitting trees. Because there are no course markers or ropes, you may enter without noticing. If you call for rescue, you may be charged large search costs, and rescue may be impossible in bad weather. Follow the resort's rules and stay out of closed areas.Local or facility rule
  • Be prepared so you don't run into bears in the mountainsIn mountains and forests, you may come across bears. Let them know people are around with a bell or your voice, don't go alone, and don't leave food, bags or rubbish behind. If you meet a bear, don't approach it. Stay calm, back away slowly while watching it, and don't turn your back and run.Good to know
  • Don't eat while walking in crowded placesEating while walking is seen as impolite in most public places. In narrow or crowded streets, you may also soil other people's clothes.Good to know
  • Do not take the hotel's room items homeSome things in hotel and ryokan rooms are the property of the accommodation and must not be taken home. If you are not sure whether you may take something, ask the staff.Good to know
  • Get travel insurance, and always pay your hospital billsTravelers who are not covered by Japan's public health insurance may have to pay the full cost of hospital treatment themselves. Before your trip, take out travel insurance with sufficient coverage. Some travel insurance can be purchased even after arriving in Japan. If you leave medical bills unpaid, you may face stricter immigration screening the next time you come to Japan, and may even be refused entry.Good to know
  • Keep the noise down at night in vacation rentalsVacation rentals (民泊) are often in residential areas where people live. By law, hosts must explain to guests what to keep in mind to avoid noise. Follow the rules you are given, and stay quiet at night and early in the morning.Good to know
  • Be considerate of people and places in photosAsk before photographing people you don't know. Don't enter private land or go past fences for a photo, and follow no-photography signs.Good to know
  • Keep quiet in residential areas and don't photograph residents without askingEven right next to tourist spots, there are residential streets where people live. They are the setting of residents' daily lives. Talking loudly, stopping and blocking the street, or photographing residents and children without asking disturbs their lives and can make them feel uneasy. Do not enter private property without permission, even to photograph the scenery or a building. When a map app routes you through a residential street as a shortcut, pass through quietly.Good to know
  • Be quiet and respectful at shrines and templesShrines and temples are places of worship. Behave quietly and follow the signs and instructions. If you are unsure how to pray, simply following those around you is fine.Good to know
  • Carry your trash if there is no binPublic trash cans are limited in Japan. Sort your trash if you find a bin. If you don't, carry it with you and dispose of it later.Good to know
  • At the sea or a river, do not swim where it is prohibited, and wear a life jacketAt the sea, swim at beaches with lifeguards or monitors, inside the designated swimming area. Never swim where swimming is prohibited. Check the wind direction and wave height, and do not enter the water near river mouths, along breakwaters or around rocks, where strong currents flowing out to sea (rip currents) tend to form. Rivers can look calm but have uneven currents, and the water can rise suddenly when it rains upstream. Near water, wear a life jacket, keep your eyes on children, and do not play in the water when you feel unwell.Good to know
  • Don't feed wild animalsIn national parks and elsewhere, you are asked not to feed wild animals. In national and quasi-national parks, there are places where feeding wild animals, or getting very close to them or following them, is regulated by law. Leaving food or rubbish behind also attracts animals.Good to know
  • Wear a yukata with the left side over the rightRyokan and hot spring inns often provide a yukata in your room. Fold the left side of the yukata over the right side, and tie the obi belt at the front of your waist.Good to know
