Emergencies & disasters

  • Evacuate early when an evacuation alert is issuedEvacuation information is issued by the mayor of the municipality. Be sure to evacuate by Alert Level 4, the evacuation order (hinan shiji). At Level 3, older people and others who need more time evacuate. Even with no alert, if you sense danger, decide for yourself and evacuate.Local or facility rule
  • Check evacuation sites on a hazard mapThe national Hazard Map Portal links to each municipality's hazard maps; the site is in Japanese only. Designated emergency evacuation sites are where you flee when a disaster strikes, and designated shelters are where you stay for a while. They are different.Local or facility rule
  • Call 110 for crime and accidents, 119 for fire and ambulanceCall the police on 110 for crimes and accidents, and the fire department on 119 for fires, sudden illness and injuries. For non-emergency police consultation, call #9110. When calling for an ambulance, first say "kyukyu desu" (it's an ambulance call).Good to know
  • If someone collapses, call for help and ask people to dial 119 and bring an AEDIf you see someone suddenly collapse or not respond, first check that the area is safe, then shout for help, for example "Someone come! A person has collapsed!" When people come, give each a clear task, such as "Please call 119" and "Please bring an AED". Even if you do not know what to do, the 119 dispatcher on the phone will tell you how to help. Putting the phone on speaker keeps both hands free. AEDs are placed in stations, airports, public facilities and elsewhere, and once opened, they guide you with voice messages and lights.Good to know
  • Where to ask if you're unsure about calling an ambulanceIf you can't decide whether to call an ambulance, some areas offer phone advice on #7119 or another number. It isn't available everywhere, and the number and hours differ by area. Check whether advice is available in other languages. If symptoms seem life-threatening, call 119 without hesitation.Good to know
  • Use the Disaster Message Dial 171 to reach familyIn a major disaster, phone lines get congested and the 171 service lets you leave voice messages. It isn't on in normal times, its voice guidance is in Japanese only, and it can't be used from overseas. web171 lets you leave text messages. Avoid phone calls where possible and use data.Good to know
  • When the ground shakes, protect yourself firstWhen you see or hear an earthquake early warning, or feel shaking, stay calm and protect yourself first. Indoors, protect your head and take cover under a sturdy desk or similar. Do not rush outside, and do not force yourself to go and put out a fire. Outdoors, watch out for falling block walls, signs and broken glass. In an elevator, stop at the nearest floor and get out. On trains and buses, hold on firmly to a strap or handrail.Good to know
  • Watch out for heatstroke on hot daysHeatstroke can happen indoors and at night, and it can be life-threatening. When a heatstroke alert is issued, drink water and take salt before you feel thirsty, use your air conditioner properly, and avoid going out unless necessary. People around older people and others at risk should check on them.Good to know
