Smoking & drinking

  • Do not bother others when drunkA Japanese law prohibits drunk people from behaving in a grossly rude or violent way that bothers others in public places or on public transport, such as streets, stations and trains. Violations can lead to short detention or a petty fine. The same law asks people not to force others to drink.Law
  • No drinking under 20The age of adulthood in Japan is 18, but the age limit for alcohol stays at 20. The law prohibits drinking under 20, and businesses may not sell or serve alcohol to anyone under 20.Law
  • Never drive after drinkingThe Road Traffic Act prohibits driving under the influence of alcohol, regardless of the amount. Not only the driver but also those who lend the car, serve the alcohol or ride along can be punished.Law
  • Indoor spaces are smoke-free in principleUnder the Health Promotion Act, indoor areas of facilities used by many people, including restaurants, are smoke-free in principle. Smoking is allowed in smoking rooms that meet the requirements. Schools, hospitals, child welfare facilities and government offices are in principle smoke-free across the whole site. People under 20 may not enter smoking areas.Law
  • No smoking under 20The age of adulthood in Japan is 18, but the age limit for tobacco stays at 20. The law prohibits smoking under 20.Law
  • Don't drink on the street around Shibuya Station at nightUnder a Shibuya City ordinance, drinking alcohol at night in public places such as streets and parks within a designated area around Shibuya Station is prohibited all year round. Check the area and hours on the map and guidance on the city's page.Local or facility ruleApplies in: Around Shibuya Station, Shibuya City, Tokyo
  • Some areas ban smoking on the streetSome municipalities ban smoking on the street by ordinance and charge an administrative fine for violations. For example, the ban covers all of Chiyoda, Tokyo (except the Imperial Palace) and all of Osaka City. Zones and amounts differ by municipality.Local or facility rule
