Crime & the law

  • Do not sell or give away your bank accountSelling or handing over a bank account, cash card, passbook or login details is a crime. It can be a crime even if you receive no money. Close accounts you will no longer use, such as when you leave Japan, at your bank.Law
  • Don't sell counterfeit brand goods or order them from abroadSelling counterfeit goods made to look like a real brand infringes trademark rights and is punishable under the Trademark Act. Selling them on flea market apps or social media is the same. Counterfeit goods sent by businesses overseas are subject to confiscation at customs, even if they are for your own use. If an item is suspiciously cheap, check that it is genuine before buying.Law
  • Check your license is valid in Japan before drivingTo drive a car or motorcycle in Japan you need a Japanese license, an International Driving Permit under the treaty, or a foreign license with a designated translation. There is a limit on how long these can be used, and the eligible countries are set. Driving without a license is unlicensed driving.Law
  • Stay away from cannabis and other illegal drugsIn Japan, the law prohibits not only possessing or transferring cannabis but also using it. Sweets and other foods containing cannabis ingredients are sold overseas, and people have been arrested trying to bring them into Japan as souvenirs. Also stay away from products sold with claims such as 'legal'.Law
  • Groping is a serious crimeGroping people, such as on trains, is a serious crime that tramples on personal dignity. It is punished under local ordinances and the Penal Code. If this happens to you, do not hesitate to call 110.Law
  • Do not carry bladesThe law prohibits carrying blades without a legitimate reason such as work. Self-defense is not a legitimate reason. Even stationery such as scissors or box cutters can lead to police action if carried ready for use without a legitimate reason. Hiding a blade in a car can also count as carrying it.Law
  • Do not take other people's things, even an umbrellaTaking someone else's things left on a seat, a luggage rack or an umbrella stand is a crime such as theft, even if the owner has stepped away. The same applies to cheap things like umbrellas. If you do not know who owns something, hand it in to staff or the police as a found item.Law
  • Do not keep what you findIf you find something someone has lost, return it to the owner or hand it in to the police. If you find it inside a station, shop or similar facility, give it to the staff. Keeping it may lead to a charge of embezzling lost property.Law
  • Don't sell or give away medicine you brought from your home countrySelling or giving medicines to others without a license is prohibited under the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act. Selling them on social media or flea market apps is the same. Even selling leftover medicine just once can be illegal. Medicines you brought in or ordered from abroad for your own use are only for you, and you may not sell or give them even to family or friends. If someone is unwell, suggest a clinic or pharmacy.Law
  • Sexual acts without consent are crimesEngaging in a sexual act when the other person finds it difficult to form, express or maintain the will to refuse is punished as non-consensual indecency or non-consensual sexual intercourse. This includes situations such as being under the influence of alcohol or drugs, being asleep, being frozen with fear, or being unable to refuse because of a difference in position. It is punished whether or not the two people are married. Sexual acts with children under 16 are also punished (if the child is 13 or older, this applies to people who are at least five years older).Law
  • Do not use someone else's bicycle without permissionDo not ride or take someone else's bicycle without permission. Even if a bicycle looks abandoned, taking it without permission is the crime of embezzling lost property.Law
  • Do not hand over a contracted phone without permissionHanding a contracted mobile phone to someone without the phone company's consent can be punished as a violation of the law. Signing a contract with no intention of using it, then handing over the phone for money, is fraud against the shop. When you will stop using it, such as when you leave Japan, contact the phone company and cancel.Law
  • Don't disturb people or businesses for videos or live streamsCausing a disturbance or playing pranks in shops, on trains and so on to film or stream videos can be the crime of obstruction of business under the Penal Code if it interferes with people's work. Filming strangers without asking and streaming it may violate their portrait rights and privacy. When filming, follow the rules of the shop or facility and get permission from the people who appear.Law
  • Do not give away your residence card or use someone else'sThere are penalties for providing your own residence card to someone else, for using, receiving or holding a card in another person's name, and for involvement with forged cards. Foreign nationals who do these things, or who encourage or help others to do them, also fall under the grounds for deportation.Law
  • Do not secretly film people's bodies or underwearSecretly filming a person's sexual body parts or the underwear they are wearing, without a legitimate reason, is a crime. Giving the images to others or making them public is also punishable.Law
  • Shoplifting is a crime, even for small amountsTaking goods from a shop without paying is shoplifting, which falls under the crime of theft. Small amounts are treated as the same crime. Returning the goods or paying for them afterwards does not settle the matter.Law
  • Violence is a crime, even if no one is injuredUsing violence against someone is the crime of assault even if they are not injured, and the crime of causing injury if they are. Even in a fight over drinks, hitting someone is a crime.Law
  • Do not answer 'easy, high-paying' criminal job adsSo-called yami-baito ads use words like 'high pay', 'same-day cash' or 'just collect some documents' to recruit people to carry out crimes. The ID you send when applying can be used to threaten you, with words like 'we will come to your home' or 'we will harm your family', and you may not be able to quit. If you are approached or have already applied, talk to the police right away.Law
  • One person's crime can turn into blame on all foreign residentsA crime committed by one foreign national does not always stay that person's problem. It can spread into blame on all the foreign residents who follow the rules, in words like 'foreigners are...'. This does not mean the blame is right. Every country has people who commit crimes and people who live by the law. Following the law protects not only you but also the people you live with. The Ministry of Justice runs a human rights consultation service for people who face discrimination.Good to know
