Restaurants & convenience stores

  • Don't stand chopsticks in rice or pass food chopstick to chopstickSticking chopsticks upright in rice (tatebashi) and passing food from chopsticks to chopsticks (hashiwatashi) are avoided because they resemble funeral customs.Manners
  • At banquets, order what you can finishThe Ministry of the Environment introduces efforts such as the '3010 campaign' to reduce leftovers at banquets. This is an appeal, not a rule, and the name and content vary by region. Order a suitable amount and keep 'mottainai', the spirit of not wasting, in mind.Manners
  • No need to tipTipping is not customary in Japan and will often be met with confusion.Manners
  • Otoshi at an izakaya may cost extraAt an izakaya, you may be served a small dish called otoshi, and it may be charged separately. If you are concerned, ask the staff.Manners
  • Do not open products before you payIn Japanese stores, you pay at the register before you open a product. Even if you plan to pay later, opening, eating or drinking something before checkout is seen as bad manners in Japan. It may be no problem in some countries, but people in Japan take it especially seriously.Manners
  • Line up at registers and restaurantsPeople line up for everything in Japan: trains, checkouts, restaurants, store openings and more.Manners
  • Arrive on time, or cancel earlyShow up on time for a reservation. If you can't make it, cancel well in advance.Manners
  • Don't put household garbage in store binsSome municipalities ask people not to put household garbage in the bins at convenience stores and businesses. This example is from Higashihiroshima City, Hiroshima.Manners
