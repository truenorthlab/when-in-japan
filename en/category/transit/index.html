Trains & buses

  • Don't ride in unlicensed taxis (shirotaku) or carry paying passengers yourselfCarrying passengers for money in a private car without government permission is called "shirotaku" (白タク) and is prohibited under the Road Transport Act. You may be approached at airports or tourist spots, or such rides may be arranged through ride apps. In an unlicensed taxi, you may not receive proper compensation if there is an accident. Use licensed taxis or authorized services.Law
  • Stand still on escalatorsSome municipalities have ordinances requiring people to stand still on escalators, on either side. The link is one prefecture's example. Follow the notices at stations and facilities.Local or facility rule
  • Check the signs for women-only carsSome railways have women-only cars. Hours and lines differ by company, and they may not run depending on service conditions. On Tokyo Metro, for example, elementary school children and younger, people with disabilities and their caregivers may also ride.Local or facility rule
  • Take off your backpack on crowded trainsOn crowded trains, you are asked to hold backpacks and other bags in front of you, carry them by hand or put them on the overhead rack.Good to know
  • How you pay on a bus depends on the routeOn buses where the fare depends on distance, take a numbered ticket when you board, then check the number on the board and pay when you get off. On buses with a flat fare, you pay when you board. Some buses do not accept IC cards, and some machines do not give change.Good to know
  • On buses, wait until the bus stops before you stand upOn local buses, passengers fall and get hurt when the bus shakes as it starts, stops or brakes suddenly. Many of those hurt are older people. Even as your stop gets close, stay seated until the bus has come to a complete stop. When standing, hold on firmly to a handrail or strap.Good to know
  • Keep your earphones from leaking soundListening to loud music on trains and buses disturbs others. Keep sound from leaking out of your earphones, and do not play sound through your phone's speaker.Good to know
  • Avoid eating and drinking on crowded trainsIn a survey by a railway association, eating and drinking on crowded trains is listed among behaviors people find annoying. It is not a rule, but avoid it when the train is crowded.Good to know
  • Do not talk loudly on trains and busesTalking loudly or making noise on trains and buses disturbs others. When you talk, keep your voice low so you do not bother people around you.Good to know
  • Don't do your makeup on the trainIn a survey by an association of railway companies, putting on makeup on the train is listed as one of the behaviors people find annoying. Strong scents, such as from cosmetics, are also listed. It is not a rule, but do your makeup at home or in a restroom.Good to know
  • Don't take phone calls on trains and busesPassengers are asked to set phones to silent mode and refrain from calls. Near priority seats, some railways ask you to turn your phone off when the car is crowded.Good to know
  • Stay behind the yellow line on platformsNear the edge of a platform you can be hit by a train or fall onto the tracks. Walk and wait on the inner side of the yellow tactile paving, which is there for people with visual impairments.Good to know
  • Offer your seat to those who need itOffer your seat to older people, people with disabilities or internal conditions, people with small children and pregnant people. The need isn't always visible, and this isn't limited to priority seats.Good to know
  • Let people off first, then board in lineWhen boarding a train, passengers getting off go first. Wait in line on the platform and board in the order you lined up.Good to know
  • Do not rush onto trains as the doors closeRunning onto a train as the doors are closing is dangerous: you can get caught in the doors or bump into people on the platform. Leave enough time to get to the station, and if you miss a train, wait for the next one.Good to know
  • Do not lie down on seats; take only one seatLying down on seats or stretching or spreading your legs keeps others from sitting and disturbs them. Sit close together, and keep your bags on your lap or on the luggage rack.Good to know
  • Be kind to people with strollers on trains and busesThe stroller mark shows places and equipment where people with strollers can feel safe, such as elevators and the spaces inside trains and buses. Places such as escalators have a no-stroller mark, meaning strollers must not be used there. People nearby are asked to be understanding, and to offer help where someone seems to be struggling, such as where there is no elevator. People with strollers also take care not to bump into others or block the way, and can ask for help without hesitation when they need it.Good to know
  • Do not open or close taxi doors yourselfThe rear left door of a taxi opens and closes automatically, and as a rule the rear right door is not opened or closed. You also do not need to tip.Good to know
  • Do not use your phone while walking in stationsWalking while using a smartphone or game device on platforms and in station passages can make you bump into people or fall from the platform. Stop somewhere out of the way before you use it.Good to know
