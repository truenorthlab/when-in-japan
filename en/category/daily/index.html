Everyday manners

  • Do not spit or relieve yourself in the streetThe Minor Offenses Act (軽犯罪法) prohibits spitting and urinating or defecating in streets, parks and other places where people gather. Violations can lead to short detention or a petty fine.Legal rule
  • Check the park's rules before using fireworksWhether you can use fireworks in a park depends on the municipality and the park. Even where they are allowed there are conditions such as having water ready and taking your trash home, and aerial or noisy fireworks may be banned. The link is one city's example. Check the park's notices or the municipality's website.Local or facility rule
  • Cover your mouth and nose when you cough or sneezeWhen you cough or sneeze, cover your mouth and nose with a mask, tissue or handkerchief. If there is no time, use your sleeve or the inside of your elbow. Coughing or sneezing without regard for others is also listed among behaviors people find annoying on trains.Manners
  • Start with a light bowA light nod or bow when you meet someone or enter or leave a shop comes across as polite. You do not need to memorize detailed rules of bowing. Exchanging greetings with your neighbors day to day makes trouble less likely.Manners
  • For minor illness or injury, go to a nearby clinicMedical institutions in Japan have different roles, and for minor illness or injury you first go to a nearby clinic. If you do not show your My Number health insurance card or similar proof, you pay the full medical costs yourself. If you cannot speak Japanese, you can ask your municipality, an international exchange association or an NPO for help.Manners
  • Mind your voice and how much space you take up in crowded placesTalking loudly in crowded places such as streets and station passages, or blocking the way in a large group, is seen as a nuisance. Talking itself is not the problem, and a soft voice is fine.Manners
  • Flush your toilet paperWith Japanese flush toilets, use the paper provided and flush it after use. Some countries have a custom of putting used paper in a bin, but the provided paper rarely clogs the toilet here. Where there are dedicated toilet slippers, change into them.Manners
  • Hold your umbrella so it does not hit peopleOn rainy days, a wet umbrella that touches people or a tip pointed at the person behind you is a nuisance and can be dangerous. Hold a closed umbrella close to your body with the tip pointing down.Manners
