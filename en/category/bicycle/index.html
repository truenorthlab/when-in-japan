Bicycles

  • Don't ride a bicycle after drinkingThe Road Traffic Act says no one may drive a vehicle under the influence of alcohol, and this includes bicycles. Not only the rider but also those who provide the bicycle or alcohol, and those riding along, can be punished.Legal rule
  • Ride bicycles on the left side of the roadAs a rule, bicycles use the roadway and keep to the left. Sidewalks are the exception, and pedestrians come first. You may use a sidewalk when signs allow it, if you are under 13 or 70 or older, if a physical disability makes the roadway difficult, or when it is unavoidable for safety.Legal rule
  • Don't use your phone while cyclingHolding a phone to talk or staring at the screen while riding a bicycle is prohibited by the Road Traffic Act, with penalties and a fine system (the "blue ticket"). Using it while stopped is not covered. See the official page for details.Legal rule
  • Register your bicycle for crime preventionBy law, people who use a bicycle must have it registered for crime prevention (bohan toroku). This also applies to used or hand-me-down bicycles. Where to register depends on the area; in Tokyo, for example, it is done at authorized bicycle shops.Legal rule
  • Don't ride a bicycle holding an umbrella, with a passenger or side by sideRiding a bicycle while holding an umbrella, riding with earphones so that you can't hear the sounds around you, carrying a passenger, riding side by side, and riding without a light at night are prohibited by law or prefectural rules, and are subject to fines under the “blue ticket” system. There are allowed exceptions, such as carrying a small child in a child seat.Legal rule
  • Park bicycles only where allowedSome municipalities remove abandoned bicycles. In Shinjuku, Tokyo, for example, bicycles left in no-parking zones can be removed immediately, and there is a fee to get them back. Even outside those zones, you may not simply leave a bicycle.Local or facility rule
