Good to know

Don't feed wild animals

In national parks and elsewhere, you are asked not to feed wild animals. In national and quasi-national parks, there are places where feeding wild animals, or getting very close to them or following them, is regulated by law. Leaving food or rubbish behind also attracts animals.

Related items

  • Don't catch wild birds or animalsWild birds and mammals are, as a rule, protected under the Wildlife Protection and Management Act, and catching or harming them without permission is prohibited. Taking bird eggs is also prohibited. This includes familiar birds in towns and parks, such as pigeons, sparrows and crows. Removing a nest with eggs or chicks in it is also prohibited. Do not catch them to eat or keep as pets; just watch them.Law
  • Don't take plants or stones from national parksIn special zones of national parks and quasi-national parks, the Natural Parks Act restricts picking designated plants, taking stones or soil, and other activities. You cannot tell by looking which places are restricted, so do not take flowers, branches or stones, and stay on the marked paths.Law
