Crime & the law

Manners

One person's crime can turn into blame on all foreign residents

A crime committed by one foreign national does not always stay that person's problem. It can spread into blame on all the foreign residents who follow the rules, in words like 'foreigners are...'. This does not mean the blame is right. Following the law protects not only you but also the people you live with. The Ministry of Justice runs a human rights consultation service for people who face discrimination.

Machine translation

Do

  • Do not brush things off as minor. Check the rules in Japan
  • If someone invites you into something illegal, refuse and contact an official service
  • If you face discrimination, use the human rights consultation. Languages are limited, so check the link

Avoid

  • Get involved in something illegal because a friend asked or it seemed harmless
  • Think 'everyone does it' or 'no one will find out'

  • One person's bad manners can shape how all foreign residents are seenRules and manners for daily life differ from country to country. Most bad manners happen because people did not know things are different in Japan, or simply slipped up. Even so, easily noticed behavior such as talking loudly on trains, cutting in line or putting out garbage the wrong way is sometimes covered in the news and on social media. It can turn into views like 'foreigners are...', and all the foreign residents who follow the rules can end up being looked at harshly. This does not mean those views are right. Original Japanese

一人の犯罪が、外国人みんなへの非難に広がることがある

一人の外国人が起こした犯罪は、その人だけの問題で終わらず、「外国人は…」という言葉で、ルールを守って暮らす外国人みんなへの非難に広がることがあります。その非難が正しいという意味ではありません。法律を守ることは、自分だけでなく、一緒に暮らす人たちを守ることにもつながります。差別を受けたときのために、法務省の人権相談窓口があります。
