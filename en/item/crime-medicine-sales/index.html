Crime & the law

Law

Don't sell or give away medicine you brought from your home country

Selling or giving medicines to others without a license is prohibited under the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act. Selling them on social media or flea market apps is the same. Even selling leftover medicine just once can be illegal. Medicines you brought in or ordered from abroad for your own use are only for you, and you may not sell or give them even to family or friends. If someone is unwell, suggest a clinic or pharmacy.

Related items

  • Check the rules before bringing medicineSome medicines brought from abroad have quantity limits or need procedures in advance. Amphetamines and cannabis cannot be brought in, even for medical treatment.Law
  • Stay away from cannabis and other illegal drugsIn Japan, the law prohibits not only possessing or transferring cannabis but also using it. Sweets and other foods containing cannabis ingredients are sold overseas, and people have been arrested trying to bring them into Japan as souvenirs. Also stay away from products sold with claims such as 'legal'.Law
  • For minor illness or injury, go to a nearby clinicMedical institutions in Japan have different roles, and for minor illness or injury you first go to a nearby clinic. If you do not show your My Number health insurance card or similar proof, you pay the full medical costs yourself. If you cannot speak Japanese, you can ask your municipality, an international exchange association or an NPO for help.Good to know
Original Japanese

母国から持ってきた薬を売ったり、人に渡したりしない

許可なく医薬品を売ったり人に渡したりすることは、医薬品医療機器等法で禁止されています。SNSやフリマアプリで売ることも同じです。余った薬を一度だけ売る場合でも、違法になることがあります。自分で使うために持ち込んだり取り寄せたりした薬は、本人が使うためのもので、家族や友人であっても売ったり渡したりできません。具合が悪い人には、病院や薬局を勧めます。
