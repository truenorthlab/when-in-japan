Travel & sightseeing

Law

Don't enter off-limits places, other people's fields or private roads

Entering a place where entry is prohibited, or someone else's fields, without a legitimate reason is prohibited under the Minor Offenses Act. Entering someone's home without permission can also be the crime of trespassing under the Penal Code. Some roads that anyone can walk on are private roads owned by individuals or others rather than the national or local government. Follow any signs closing the road or prohibiting photography. Even for photos or a shortcut, do not go in when there is a sign.

Machine translation

Do

  • Look for signs such as "No entry", "Private property" or "Private road"
  • If you are not sure you may enter, turn back instead

Avoid

  • Climb over fences or ropes for a photo or a shortcut
  • Walk into fields, rice paddies or pastures
  • Stop on a private road to take photos or hang around

Do not stop, touch, follow or photograph geiko and maiko without permission as they walk by. Photographing on private property such as in front of shops, and entering it without permission, have also become problems. This is a place where people live their daily lives, not a theme park. Soil carried on your shoes can also harm the crops. Do not drive or walk onto gravel roads or farm roads either. Original Japanese

立入禁止の場所や他人の畑、私道に勝手に入らない

入ることを禁じられた場所や他人の田畑に、正当な理由なく入ることは、軽犯罪法で禁止されています。人の家などに勝手に入ると、刑法の住居侵入罪になることもあります。誰でも通れる道でも、国や自治体ではなく個人などが持っている「私道」があります。通行止めや撮影禁止の表示があれば従います。写真や近道のためでも、表示があれば入りません。
