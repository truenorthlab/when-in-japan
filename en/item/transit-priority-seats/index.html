Trains & buses

Good to know

Offer your seat to those who need it

Offer your seat to older people, people with disabilities or internal conditions, people with small children and pregnant people. The need isn't always visible, and this isn't limited to priority seats.

Machine translation

Do

  • Offer your seat to anyone who seems to need it
  • Look out for people wearing a Help Mark

Avoid

  • Assume from appearance that someone doesn't need a seat

Related items

  • Know what the Help Mark and the Maternity Mark meanThe Help Mark is worn by people who need help or consideration even when it does not show, such as people with artificial limbs or joints, internal disabilities or intractable diseases, or in early pregnancy. The Maternity Mark lets people nearby know that the wearer is pregnant. When you see one, you are asked to act with consideration, such as offering your seat on trains and buses, or speaking to the person if they seem to need help.Good to know
  • Be kind to people with strollers on trains and busesThe stroller mark shows places and equipment where people with strollers can feel safe, such as elevators and the spaces inside trains and buses. Places such as escalators have a no-stroller mark, meaning strollers must not be used there. People nearby are asked to be understanding, and to offer help where someone seems to be struggling, such as where there is no elevator. People with strollers also take care not to bump into others or block the way, and can ask for help without hesitation when they need it.Good to know
