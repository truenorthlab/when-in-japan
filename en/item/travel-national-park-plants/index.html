Travel & sightseeing

Law

Don't take plants or stones from national parks

In special zones of national parks and quasi-national parks, the Natural Parks Act restricts picking designated plants, taking stones or soil, and other activities. You cannot tell by looking which places are restricted, so do not take flowers, branches or stones, and stay on the marked paths.

Related items

  • Don't feed wild animalsIn national parks and elsewhere, you are asked not to feed wild animals. In national and quasi-national parks, there are places where feeding wild animals, or getting very close to them or following them, is regulated by law. Leaving food or rubbish behind also attracts animals.Good to know
  • Don't catch wild birds or animalsWild birds and mammals are, as a rule, protected under the Wildlife Protection and Management Act, and catching or harming them without permission is prohibited. Taking bird eggs is also prohibited. This includes familiar birds in towns and parks, such as pigeons, sparrows and crows. Removing a nest with eggs or chicks in it is also prohibited. Do not catch them to eat or keep as pets; just watch them.Law
  • Carry your trash if there is no binPublic trash cans are limited in Japan. Sort your trash if you find a bin. If you don't, carry it with you and dispose of it later.Good to know
Original Japanese

国立公園などで植物や石を持ち帰らない

国立公園や国定公園の特別地域などでは、自然公園法で、指定された植物を採ることや、石や土を採ることなどが規制されています。どこが規制されているかは見た目では分からないので、花や枝、石を持ち帰らず、決められた道を歩きます。
